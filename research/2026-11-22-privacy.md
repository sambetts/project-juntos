# Privacy and safety groundwork, 2026-11-22 (B-013)

(Legal & Compliance.) **For the free app (D-048), the data map, DPIA and record of processing are in `research/2026-11-28-dpia.md`; this note covers the hand-run pilot.** A working document for the Phase 1 pilot (`product/phase1-validation-plan.md`) and the waitlist on the landing-page draft (B-007). It's research to prepare the lawyer's review (F-006, Part A2 and A4), not legal advice. Everything here is a proposal until the lawyer answers and Sam approves. Legal texts were checked on 2026-11-22 against the BOE's consolidated copies and the AEPD's site (links at the end).

## Summary
- **A DPIA (EIPD) looks needed, so do a short one before the pilot.** The AEPD's art. 35.4 list says a DPIA is needed "en la mayoría de los casos" when processing meets two or more of its criteria. The pilot meets at least two: profiling of habits (criterion 1), and possible health and financial-situation data (criterion 4). Doing one costs a few hours at 20 households; the lawyer confirms (A2).
- **Lawful bases, proposed:** contract or pre-contract steps (art. 6.1.b) for running the pilot people ask to join; explicit, optional consent (art. 9.2.a) for accessibility needs; consent (art. 6.1.a) for the waitlist, interviews and recordings; legitimate interest (art. 6.1.f) for safety handling and aggregate measures. No blanket consent.
- **New point for the lawyer:** any website, the waitlist page included, must show its provider's name, postal address and email (LSSI art. 10.1.a). If Sam runs it as an individual, that means publishing a home or business address. This adds weight to A1 (individual or S.L. first?).
- **Before any real data:** restricted storage and an email tool chosen by Sam, with processor terms (art. 28) and EU hosting; a layered privacy notice (LOPDGDD art. 11); a record of processing; a breach and safety log; and Sam (or a delegate) named as responder. None of it goes in this repository (R-020).
- **Gaps closed here:** a data map with retention for every purpose (section 2); the incident process (section 6); requirements for tools (section 5). The texts themselves (privacy notice, consents, terms, code of conduct) are B-020's job, in Spanish.

## 4. DPIA decision
The AEPD's list (art. 35.4) has 11 criteria; meeting two or more means a DPIA is needed "en la mayoría de los casos", and the list isn't exhaustive (Verified, AEPD PDF, 2026-11-22).

| AEPD criterion | Pilot | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Profiling or evaluating people, including habits and several aspects of personality | Matching on how people live | Yes |
| 2. Automated decisions, or processing that contributes heavily to decisions that block access to a service | Suggestions shape who gets introduced; Sam reviews them all | Arguable |
| 4. Special categories, or data revealing financial situation | Accessibility; can-fund flag | Likely |
| 7. Large scale | About 20 households | No |
| 9. Vulnerable people (including people with disabilities) | Possible, through accessibility needs | Possible |
| 10. New technologies or innovative use | Matching strangers to co-buy; an AI may later help draft suggestions | Possible |

**Recommendation:** do a short DPIA before the pilot, using the AEPD's own guidance and tool (the "Gestiona EIPD" and "Evalúa-Riesgo" tools are on the AEPD site; not opened this session). Cover: purposes and data (section 2), necessity (Annex A minimisation), risks (exposure of habits or health data, discrimination by proxy, a match misusing contact details, stalking or scams), and measures (anonymous cards, mutual consent, no ranking on sensitive fields, restricted storage, the incident process). The lawyer reviews it (A2). Owner: Legal & Compliance drafts it with made-up examples; it lives in the repository because it holds no personal data.

## 5. Tools and storage (Sam chooses; requirements only)
- **Processor terms:** every tool that holds participants' data on our behalf needs a contract meeting GDPR art. 28 (most providers publish a DPA). Art. 28.1: choose only processors with "garantías suficientes" (Verified).
- **Where data sits:** EU or EEA hosting, or a lawful transfer mechanism (GDPR chapter V); prefer EU hosting to keep it simple (Assumption on what's available).
- **The controller's own account:** the tools sit in an account the controller (Sam or the company) controls, so the controller can sign the processor terms and answer rights requests.
- **Access:** only the named responder and any delegate; two-factor sign-in; a separate restricted folder for P7.
- **Export and delete:** must be able to find, export and delete one person's data, to answer rights requests within one month (GDPR art. 12.3, extendable by two months; Verified).
- **AI tools:** no participant data in an AI session unless Sam has approved the tool's terms for it (R-020). Telling people about AI help in suggestions is an open question for the lawyer (A2, EU AI Act).
- **Minimum set for the pilot:** a form tool (sign-up and profile), a spreadsheet for matching (pseudonymised IDs), an email account, and a video tool for calls; ideally fewer, from one provider.

## 6. Incidents
**Data breach** (lost laptop, wrong email recipient, leaked sheet):
1. Contain it (revoke access, recall the email, change passwords) and pause the pilot (pilot plan, section 7, stop rule).
2. Log it in R: what happened, which data, how many people, effects, what was done. Every breach is logged, even ones not notified (art. 33.5; Verified).
3. Decide within 72 hours of knowing whether to notify the AEPD through its electronic office form. Notification is required unless the breach is unlikely to put people's rights at risk (art. 33.1; Verified). The AEPD's breach guide and its "Comunica-Brecha RGPD" tool help decide.
4. If the risk to people is high, tell them directly (art. 34).
5. Tell the lawyer, and record it in LOG.md without personal details.

**Safety incident** (harassment, pressure for money, a scam attempt, a threat):
1. Emergencies: 112.
2. The named responder replies within 24 hours (pilot plan, section 7); Sam, unless he names a delegate (F-010).
3. Pause contact between the people involved; hear both sides; remove anyone who breached the code of conduct.
4. Log it in R (P7). A serious incident pauses the pilot.
5. Lessons, without personal details, go to LOG.md and the risks file.
