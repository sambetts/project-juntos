# The problem: can people in Spain buy alone, and do they want to buy with others? (2026-11-24)
**Status:** Research, Session 27, for `project/strategy.md` (D-048). Replaces B-004's open question. Labels: Verified (primary source opened and checked) / Reported (press, a summary, or a primary seen only through search) / Assumption. No property portal pages were opened; portal indices are cited through the press.

## Summary
- **Buying alone is out of reach for most young and single-income people.** The typical home costs about 8 years of gross household income nationally (Reported) and 15 years of one salary in Madrid (Reported). Prices rose 12.2% in the year to Q2 2026 (Verified, INE). Under-35 home ownership fell from 41% (2007) to 22% (2025) (Reported, Banco de España).
- **The deposit binds most:** an average €223,000 home needs about €61,000–70,000 in cash (20% deposit plus tax and fees), which takes 21–25 years of saving alone at 20% of a young net salary, or 11–12 years each for two co-buyers and 7–8 for three (calculated below).
- **Many people must buy on one income:** 28.4% of households (5.6 million) are one person, rising to 30.6% by 2041 (Verified, INE).
- **Demand for buying with non-partners is unmeasured in Spain.** No notary, registry or survey data separates it (searched). Press in 2026 calls buying with strangers "emerging" but "not yet taken off" (Reported). Abroad, a third to over half say they would consider co-buying with a friend or relative, but only about 5–15% of buyers actually do (Reported), and no survey anywhere measures willingness to buy with strangers.
- **Sign-ups overstate buying.** habitacion.com's waiting list of 21,000–32,000 produced about 200 room sales in 2025, under 1% (Reported, company figures).

## What this means
- The affordability problem is well evidenced and widely felt. The weak link is **demand for the remedy**: that people will co-buy with others, and especially with strangers. That is what a free release must measure, and sign-ups alone won't show it (habitacion.com converts under 1%).
- Be honest about one fact that cuts the other way: the share of Spanish tenants overburdened by housing costs has fallen since 2015, though it is still well above the EU average.
- Friends and relatives are the proven form of co-buying abroad; strangers are untested everywhere.
