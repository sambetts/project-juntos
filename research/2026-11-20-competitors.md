# Competitor flows: how rivals actually work (2026-11-20)
**Status:** B-001, Research. Evidence labels: Verified / Reported / Assumption.

## Summary
- **No rival we checked publicly runs our exact sequence.** By public evidence, none lets strangers form a group first, then choose a whole home, approve a joint brief and agree exit rules from day one. habitacion.com and Yorsio both start from a room in a flat they already source. (Verified/Reported)
- **Casicasa is the closest match in concept but the weakest in delivery.** It offers a free profile (area, budget, timing), a 42-question "MAT" cohabitation questionnaire and links to outside listings showing an indicative per-person share. Legal, financing and cohabitation decisions stay off the platform, and its self-reported turnover is €5,000. (Verified/Reported)
- **habitacion.com is the scale player.** It sells a pro indiviso share with use of one room, signed before a notary and entered in the Land Registry. Buyers take its h.armony affinity test and sign an owners' pact, and it offers a buy-back if no buyer turns up. It has raised €1.7M and says it has sold 250 rooms and studios (March 2026). Its group offer is for friends or family who already know each other. (Verified/Reported)
- **Yorsio is rent-to-own of a room, not group purchase.** It uses a cohabitation profile and a fixed purchase price. It reports a 2,500–3,000 waiting list but only its "first 12 rooms" ready in 2026, and its messaging is inconsistent: "co-owner from day one" versus a rental with a purchase option. (Verified/Reported)
- **No rival publishes an exact fee for its core product.** Exits already exist elsewhere: habitacion.com's buy-back and Vivla's and Pacaso's resale services. Having an exit is not new. What could be new is the group designing it together before it chooses a home.

## Company notes

### habitacion.com
1. **Browse.** The buyer browses a catalogue of rooms in flats the company has selected, bought and refurbished. Rooms are priced at roughly €45,000–€95,000 and shown with gross rental yields. (Verified; buying and refurbishing Reported by EFE, 22 Oct 2025.)
2. **Affinity test.** The buyer takes the h.armony™ affinity test, which the company says "guarantees" the best cohabitation. The questions are not public. (Verified)
3. **Reserve and buy.** The buyer pays a deposit and signs before a notary. The share is entered in the Land Registry, and an owners' pact is "designed together". (Verified) The founder describes a co-ownership contract with cohabitation, room-use and default clauses, plus a separate service contract under which habitacion.com acts as property manager. (Reported, Consumidor Global, 20 Mar 2026)
4. **Financing.** Personal loans of 10–12 years; the founder says they do not allow mortgages for rooms, so one owner's default does not fall on the others. (Reported) Studios can be mortgaged. (Verified)
5. **Living or letting.** The owner can live in the room or let it out through the company's rental service. (Verified)
6. **Exit.** The company finds a buyer; if none is found within the agreed term, it buys the room back. (Verified) Other co-owners get first refusal first. (Reported, Cinco Días, May 2025)

**Groups:** "¿Vienes en grupo?" is for buying a flat with friends or family, with the company finding the home. It is not a service for matching strangers. (Verified)

**Pricing:** The founder says room price is the flat's price multiplied by the buyer's share, that the company "does not interfere", and gives example shares of 25% and 30%. (Reported) Where its margin sits is not disclosed. (Assumption: it is in the purchase, refurbishment and service fees.)

**Protected characteristics:** The founder says the target customer is aged 25–35 (Reported). Whether the h.armony test uses age or sex is unknown.

**Critic:** The Barcelona urban property chamber called the model "complex and risky". (Reported, EFE, 22 Oct 2025)

**Inconsistent claims:** The homepage shows both "+35.000" and "+20.000" registered users. (Verified)

### Yorsio
1. **Profile.** The renter fills in a cohabitation profile (routines, tidiness, home-working, visitors) and is matched with compatible flatmates, or comes with their own group. (Verified)
2. **Move in.** The renter pays a "single-access fee" covering legal advice and onboarding, plus a lower deposit than the market. (Verified)
3. **Rent and build value.** Part of each monthly payment builds savings or value towards buying, at a price fixed on day one. (Verified) Press describes a 3–5-year window to buy. (Reported, Clave Inmueble) Llavo, an affiliate comparison site, says five years. (Reported, July 2026)
4. **Exit.** The renter can buy, get savings back, move them to another Yorsio home, or sell or rent out their share. (Verified)

**Messaging conflict:** The homepage says customers become co-owners "without mortgages or banks". The how-it-works page describes a rental with a purchase option. Which applies on the first day is unclear. (Verified)

**Supply:** The company reports "first 12 rooms ready to market" in 2026. (Reported, Lanzadera)

**Protected characteristics:** No protected traits are seen in the profile. Marketing targets "young people", but no age limit is stated. (Verified)

### Casicasa
1. **Profile.** The user creates a free profile (email or Google) setting area, budget and timing. (Verified)
2. **Questionnaire.** The user answers the 42-question MAT, which is presented as a prompt for conversations, with no compatibility guarantee. (Verified)
3. **Listings.** The user views a limited preview of outside listings, each linked to its source portal, with an indicative per-person price that excludes taxes and buying costs. (Verified)
4. **Groups.** "Limited recommendations only for prepared private groups." (Verified) How a private group forms, and whether strangers can meet, sits behind a login and was not checked.
5. **Everything else** (legal, financing, cohabitation) stays outside the platform. (Verified)

**Positioning drift:** Older pages and LinkedIn still say "buy a room and stop paying rent". EuroQuity says it helps "friends or strangers" find "the right match to buy a home together". (Verified/Reported)

**Protected characteristics:** The docs stress inclusion "regardless of origin". Origin is a protected characteristic, so this is worth noting, although here it is an inclusion statement, not a filter. (Verified)

## Other players found
- **[Gradual Homes](https://www.gradualhomes.com/)** (Grupo Pryconsa): the customer chooses any flat, an investor buys it, and the customer moves in with a 5% deposit. 30% of rent counts towards the price; purchase window is years 3–7; Madrid, Valencia, Alicante and Málaga. Single household, not group. (Verified)
- **[Libeen](https://www.libeen.com/preguntas-frecuentes/)**: a listed SOCIMI. Customer chooses the home, Libeen buys it, about 30% of rent counts towards the price, buy in years 3–7. Fees €2,500 to join and €3,500 at completion. Requires age 18–42, which is an age-based eligibility rule. (Verified)
- **[Aparta](https://www.aparta.com/es)**: co-owns a home with the buyer. No regular payments; a use fee is paid when the co-ownership ends; exit within 10 years. (Verified; country of origin not checked)
- **[Sostre Cívic](https://sostrecivic.coop/how-does-the-model-work/?lang=en)**: Catalan housing co-operative with use rights (cesión de uso). The co-op owns the building; members pay a refundable initial contribution plus a monthly fee; existing groups of three or more can get advice. (Verified)
- **iAhorro**: mortgage broker quoted on buying with strangers. Banks usually grant one joint mortgage with joint and several liability. (Reported, [elEconomista, 24 Mar 2026](https://www.eleconomista.es/vivienda-inmobiliario/noticias/13838885/03/26/los-expertos-hipotecarios-coinciden-comprar-una-casa-entre-varios-desconocidos-ha-dejado-de-ser-una-locura.html)) It told Libre Mercado that buying with strangers to live in is uncommon and banks are wary. (Reported, [3 Apr 2026](https://www.libertaddigital.com/libremercado/2026-04-03/comprarte-una-casa-con-un-desconocido-la-ultima-y-dudosa-formula-ante-la-crisis-de-vivienda-en-espana-7380811/))
- **Trioteca**: digital mortgage broker, free to the buyer. (Reported, [Llavo](https://llavo.es/plataformas))
- **[Urbanitae](https://www.finect.com/usuario/avillanuevae/articulos/urbanitae-o-prophero-comparativa-2025)**: CNMV-regulated property crowdfunding from €500. Investment only, no use of the home. (Reported, Finect, Nov 2026)
- **Domoblock / Reental**: tokenised property investment; not regulated by the CNMV as crowdfunding platforms. (Reported, [RedInversora, Nov 2026](https://redinversora.com/recursos/inversion-alternativa/comparativa))

## What this means for our differentiation

**Does any rival already do all three (group first, joint brief, exit from day one)?** No, not on public evidence.
- habitacion.com covers matching, a pact and an exit, but in the opposite order: home first, then co-buyers. The buyer chooses a room, not a whole home with others.
- Yorsio is a room rental with a purchase option.
- Casicasa covers the early steps (profile, questionnaire, outside listings, private groups) but no joint brief, agreement or exit service is visible.
- Vivla and Pacaso are holiday products where the operator picks the home.

**What is genuinely different**
- Strangers commit as a group before any home exists.
- A whole home is bought together, with unequal shares across mixed households. No rival publicly shows mixed households or unequal shares chosen by the group.
- A joint "what our home needs" brief that the group approves.
- An exit the group designs itself, rather than an operator buy-back.

**What isn't different**
- Affinity questionnaires (h.armony, Yorsio's profile, Casicasa's MAT).
- Linking out to listings (Casicasa does it already).
- Pro indiviso with a notarised pact (habitacion.com).
- Exit mechanisms (habitacion.com, Vivla, Pacaso).

**What we would have to prove**
1. Strangers will commit to a group before seeing a home. Casicasa's thin traction is a warning sign.
2. Banks will lend to such groups. iAhorro says lenders are wary, and habitacion.com avoids mortgages altogether.
3. Groups survive the time it takes to find a home.
4. Our matching avoids protected characteristics.

**Recommendations for the CEO**
- *Recommendation:* Treat Casicasa as the direct rival and habitacion.com as the benchmark. Watch whether habitacion.com's "en grupo" service opens to strangers.
- *Recommendation:* Reframe the claim from "we have exits" to "your group agrees exit and buy-out rules before you choose a home".
- *Recommendation:* Run a cheap test of group-first commitment, for example a refundable deposit from a matched group before any listing is shown. That is the riskiest assumption. *(Superseded for Phase 1 on 2026-11-20: no money changes hands, and a deposit needs the lawyer's clearance first; lawyer brief A5.)*
- *Recommendation:* Get a broker or bank partner's view on lending to groups of strangers before building the legal layer.
- *Recommendation:* Publish exact fees from launch. No rival does, so transparency is a cheap differentiator.

## Open questions
- What does h.armony actually ask, and does it use age, sex or nationality?
- What are habitacion.com's buy-back term and price formula, and what are its fees?
- Does a Yorsio tenant own a share on the first day or only hold an option? How much is the access fee?
- How do Casicasa's "private groups" form (behind the login), and can strangers meet there?
- Is Pacaso active in Spain at all? Its Spain page contradicts itself.
- Do any rivals have completed whole-home purchases by groups of strangers? None were found.
