## 1. The service in brief
We're exploring a service, for Spain only, that helps people who can't afford a home alone to buy one together with compatible people they haven't met.

- **How it would work:** people create a profile (how they live, what the home needs, area and timing). We suggest introductions, and both sides must accept. Matched households (two to four per group; pairs only in the pilot) agree a joint brief of what their home needs, and discuss exit rules, *before* choosing a home.
- **Stage:** idea and pilot design. No company yet, nothing public, no users, no revenue.
- **Phase 1 pilot (Part A is about this):** free and hand-run, in one Madrid area, with about 20 households (Annex B). We would **not** handle money, charge anyone, show property listings, contact agencies, sellers, banks or brokers, or give legal, tax or financial advice.
- **Later (Part B is about this):** helping groups find a home through partner agencies. Possible revenue: a disclosed lead fee from agencies, a coordination fee paid by buyers, or a fixed-fee legal pack delivered by independent lawyers. The default structure we're studying is co-ownership in shares (*pro indiviso*) with a co-ownership agreement.
- **What similar companies do** (public sources, *Reported*; our competitor note of 2026-11-20): habitacion.com sells a *pro indiviso* share with the use of one room, with a notarised owners' pact and a separate service contract. Vivla's owners hold shares in a company that owns the home; Pacaso uses one company per home. Casicasa offers profiles, an affinity test and links to outside listings.

## 3. Part A: questions before the pilot

**A1. Our status**
- Does the free pilot, as described, count as property intermediation? In particular, does it bring anti-money-laundering duties under Ley 10/2010 (art. 2.1(l)), or a duty to register as an estate agent? Our understanding: Madrid's register is voluntary (Decree 8/2018); Catalonia's and Valencia's are mandatory.
- What would change that: charging a fee, introducing groups to agencies, sellers or banks, or showing listings?
- Can the founder run the pilot as an individual, or should a company (S.L.) come first? Who is the data controller in each case? What insurance (civil liability) would you recommend?

**A2. Personal data (GDPR and LOPDGDD)** (Annex A lists what we'd collect)
- Which lawful basis fits each purpose: sign-up, suggesting introductions (profiling), passing contact details to a match once both accept, follow-up questions and interviews?
- Are any fields special-category data? We think accessibility needs (lift, step-free access) may be health data. Household size and the number of children are sensitive too. Should we handle them differently, or drop them?
- Is a DPIA (*EIPD*) required for a hand-run pilot of about 20 households (GDPR art. 35 and the AEPD's list)? Does GDPR art. 22 apply when a person reviews every suggestion and both sides decide?
- Will you review our privacy notice, consent texts and participation terms (Spanish drafts to follow)? What retention period is defensible?
- What should we require of the tools we use (EU hosting, processor agreements for forms, email and video calls)? If AI tools help draft suggestions, what must we tell people, including under the EU AI Act?
- *Added 2026-11-22 (B-013, `research/2026-11-22-privacy.md`):* the pilot seems to meet two criteria on the AEPD's art. 35.4 list (profiling of habits; possible health and financial-situation data), so we plan a short DPIA. Do you agree, and will you review it? Are 12 months for an unused waiting list, and 12 months after the pilot for safety records, defensible? If accessibility needs are health data, is explicit consent (art. 9.2.a) right, or should we drop them?

**A3. Equal treatment (Ley 15/2022, arts. 17 and 20)**
- Can the pilot be limited to one area, one home price band and a purchase window, which participants choose themselves, given that socioeconomic status is a protected ground? Can we ask people to confirm they expect to be able to fund their share (yes / not sure) without asking for figures?
- Can participants say what kind of household they'd share with (another family, a couple, single adults)? Can we use that to suggest introductions, or must it stay a private choice made after an introduction?
- Can introductions show household size and the number of children, with no other details?
- Can "no pets" be a dealbreaker if assistance dogs are excluded from it? Can smoking?
- Some people may ask for women-only groups for safety. Can we offer that?
- We plan anonymous first introductions (no name, photo, age or nationality) until both sides accept, and anyone may decline for any reason. Is that enough? What else should we do?

**A4. Safety and liability**
- What duty of care and liability do we take on by introducing strangers: meetings, scams, harassment, or someone pulling out after the others have spent time or money?
- Are these safeguards enough: a code of conduct, safety guidance, video first meetings, a reporting route, a named responder and removal for breaches?
- Should we verify identity, and how, without collecting identity documents?
- What can participation terms validly exclude under consumer law (TRLGDCU)?

**A5. Commitment without money**
- Is a signed, non-binding "group pledge" safe for participants and for us, with no unintended obligation? Wording along the lines of: we intend to search together for eight weeks using our brief, and anyone can withdraw at any time.
- Our exit-rules worksheet only gives discussion prompts (how long to stay, leaving, buy-outs, valuation, costs, deadlock, death, divorce or a new partner). How do we keep it clearly short of legal advice?
- What would a **refundable** deposit from a group require: payment services rules (Real Decreto-ley 19/2018), escrow with a notary or bank, consumer law? We won't take one unless you clear it.

**A6. What we may say**
- Can recruitment copy say things like "buy a home with compatible people" or "get on the property ladder together"?
- What do advertising and unfair competition law (Ley 34/1988 and Ley 3/1991) require of such claims?
- What must a landing page with a waiting list include under the LSSI (Ley 34/2002), including cookies?
- *Added 2026-11-22 (B-013):* if the founder runs the pilot and waiting list as an individual, must the page show his postal address under LSSI art. 10.1? Does a free waiting list count as an information society service? Does that favour forming an S.L. first (A1)?

## 4. Part B: questions for later (quote only for now)
1. **Structure** for two to four unrelated households: shares plus an agreement, an S.L., or separate registered units. Set-up and running costs, lending, tax, who may live there and how easily someone can leave. How do the structures similar companies use compare?
2. **Scenarios** under shares plus an agreement: two owners buy out the third; one of three leaves; one stops paying; deadlock; an owner dies; divorce, or a new partner moving in; a forced sale (Civil Code art. 404); an owner sells to an outsider (arts. 1522 and 1524). Can the agreement add a right of first refusal?
3. **Enforceability** of a no-division pact of up to 10 years (art. 400) with a buy-out formula, a valuation method and a reserve fund. Does it bind heirs and anyone who buys a share? Can it be registered? What about lender consent and releasing a borrower?
4. **Rooms:** can exclusive use of particular rooms be agreed, and registered?
5. **Tax** at purchase (ITP; or IVA plus AJD), on buy-outs and on ending the co-ownership (AJD, ITP, IRPF, municipal *plusvalía*), including buy-outs at different values.
6. **Mortgages:** several unrelated borrowers; joint and several liability; releasing a borrower who leaves; protecting the group if one drops out before completion. What may we say or do about mortgages without becoming a credit intermediary (Ley 5/2019)?
7. **Our later role and fees:** which activities or fees (agency lead fee, buyer-paid coordination, a legal pack from independent lawyers) bring anti-money-laundering duties, register requirements or credit-intermediary rules? Any limits on fee arrangements with lawyers or agencies? What must we disclose?
8. **Before signing *arras*:** which checks (title, charges, permitted use, community rules, financing), and which protections if one buyer drops out?
9. **The agreement:** the minimum content of a co-ownership agreement; a template you'd stand behind; a fixed fee per group.
10. **Other regions:** what changes under Catalan civil law (Book Five of the Civil Code of Catalonia) before we expand there?

## 5. What we think we know (please correct us)
Checked against these sources by an independent review on 2026-11-20.

| Topic | Our understanding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Co-ownership | Each owner holds a share of the whole. Running the home goes by a majority of shares; changes to it need everyone. Each owner may sell or mortgage their own share (Civil Code arts. 392–399) | [Código Civil](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1889-4763) |
| Ending it | Any co-owner can ask to end it, unless there's a no-division pact of up to 10 years, renewable by a new agreement (art. 400). If the home is essentially indivisible and the owners don't agree to award it to one of them, who compensates the others, it's sold and the money shared (art. 404). Third parties' rights survive (art. 405) | as above |
| Sale to an outsider | The other co-owners can step into the buyer's place on the same terms within nine days of registration or, failing that, of learning of the sale (arts. 1522 and 1524). It isn't a veto | as above |
| Tax at exit | A qualifying notarised end of co-ownership that awards an indivisible home to one owner, with fair compensation, usually pays AJD rather than ITP. IRPF can still apply to a gain | [Ley ITPAJD](https://boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1993-25359); [DGT V1023-26](https://petete.tributos.hacienda.gob.es/consultas/?num_consulta=V1023-26) |
| Anti-money-laundering | Professional intermediation in property sales is covered whatever the price. The activity, not the fee's label, decides | [Ley 10/2010](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2010-6737) |
| Agent registers | Catalonia and Valencia: mandatory. Madrid: voluntary (Decree 8/2018) | [Madrid register](https://sede.comunidad.madrid/inscripciones-registro/registro-agentes-inmobiliarios) |
| Equal treatment | Covers goods and services offered to the public (art. 17) and housing, including portals and public offers (art. 20). Grounds include age, disability, health and socioeconomic status; indirect discrimination is covered | [Ley 15/2022](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-11589) |
| Privacy | A lawful basis for each purpose; clear notices; a special-category review; assess GDPR arts. 22 and 35 before profiling real people | [GDPR](http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj); [LOPDGDD](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2018-16673); [AEPD DPIA list](https://www.aepd.es/documento/listas-dpia-en-35-4.pdf) |
| Mortgages | Several borrowers can borrow jointly. Joint and several liability is common but not automatic. A lender may refuse to release a borrower who leaves | [Banco de España](https://clientebancario.bde.es/pcb/es/blog/cambio-en-la-titularidad-de-un-prestamo-hipotecario.html) |
| Credit | Check the real-estate credit law before facilitating any mortgage | [Ley 5/2019](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2019-3814) |

## 7. Annex B: the pilot in brief
