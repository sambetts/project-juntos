# Spanish legal landscape: orientation notes (2026-11-18, corrected 2026-11-20)

**Not legal advice.** Purpose: brief a Spanish property lawyer (B-003). The independent review checked these points against the BOE, the tax authority and the courts; links are in `research/2026-11-20-independent-review.md`. Anything marked *Assumption* still needs checking.

## Ways for unrelated people to own one home
1. **Co-ownership in shares (pro indiviso, Civil Code arts. 392–406).** Each buyer owns a percentage of the whole home, recorded in the deed and the Land Registry. This is the simple default.
   - Shares set each owner's part of costs and benefits; decisions on running the home go by majority of shares; changes to it need everyone's consent (arts. 393–398). Each owner may sell or mortgage their own share (art. 399).
   - Any co-owner can ask to end the co-ownership at any time, unless there's a pact to keep it undivided. A pact can last up to 10 years and is renewable by a new agreement (art. 400).
   - If the home is essentially indivisible and the owners don't agree to award it to one of them, who compensates the others, it's sold and the money shared (art. 404). Lenders' and other third parties' rights survive (art. 405).
   - If a co-owner sells their share to an outsider, the others can step into the buyer's place on the same terms, within nine days of registration or, failing that, of learning of the sale (arts. 1522 and 1524). It's not a veto.
   - A private co-ownership agreement can cover who uses which rooms, costs, decisions, deadlock, a buy-out formula, reserves, notice periods, and what happens on death, divorce or missed payments.
   - Check Catalan civil law separately before expanding to Catalonia.
2. **A company owns the home** (for example an S.L.). The buyers own company shares, not the home. Exits become share transfers. Compare tax, admin, who may live there, lending and how easily people can sell out.
3. **Separate registered units** where planning and the Land Registry allow it.

## Money
- **Mortgages:** lending to several borrowers jointly is possible. Joint and several liability is commonly reported but not automatic, so check each deed. A lender may refuse to release a borrower who leaves. How often banks lend against a single share, and on what terms for 3–4 unrelated households, is unverified (B-009).
- **Taxes at purchase:** resale homes pay ITP (regional rate). A qualifying first sale by a developer usually pays IVA (generally 10%) plus regional AJD on the deed. Check exceptions and the tax base.
- **Taxes at exit:**
  - Ending a co-ownership by notarised deed, awarding an indivisible home to one owner who pays the others fairly: usually AJD, not ITP.
  - Selling a share while the co-ownership continues, or paying over the odds: this can bring ITP. If the excess is given free, gift tax can apply.
  - A buy-out at a profit can bring income tax (IRPF) even when AJD applies.
  - Municipal plusvalía applies only when there's a taxable transfer and a gain in urban land value.

## Running the platform
- **Anti-money-laundering:** Ley 10/2010 covers professional intermediation in property sales whatever the price (rentals only above €10,000 a month or €120,000 a year). Its duties include identifying customers and beneficial owners, monitoring, reporting suspicions to SEPBLAC, keeping records for ten years and risk-based controls. The activity, not the fee's label, decides whether we're covered. Counsel should map our actual workflow (D-010).
- **Agent registers:** Catalonia and Valencia have mandatory registers. In Valencia the register survived a court's partial annulment of some requirements. Madrid has a voluntary register (Decree 8/2018); a proposal to make it mandatory isn't law.
- **Privacy:** profiles hold personal, financial and household data. Use a lawful basis for each purpose rather than blanket consent, give clear notices, review special-category data, and check whether a DPIA is needed before profiling real people (GDPR, LOPDGDD, AEPD lists).
- **Equal treatment:** Ley 15/2022 covers goods and services offered to the public (art. 17) and housing, including portals and public offers (art. 20). Its grounds include age, disability, health and socioeconomic status, and it covers indirect discrimination. Private choices differ from platform filters, and an invite-only service offered to the public isn't exempt.
- **Credit:** check the consumer credit rules (Ley 5/2019) before facilitating any mortgage.
- **Also:** consumer and advertising law for anything we claim. Listings: portal terms and the EU database right (see the homes note).

## Questions for the lawyer
1. The best structure for 2–4 unrelated households: shares plus an agreement, an S.L., or separate units? Set-up and running costs?
2. Run these scenarios under shares plus an agreement: two owners buy out the third; one of three leaves; one stops paying; deadlock; one owner dies; a forced sale.
3. How enforceable is a 10-year no-division pact plus a buy-out formula, valuation method and reserve? Does it bind heirs, or someone who buys a share? What about lender consent and release?
4. Which of our possible activities and fee models bring Ley 10/2010 duties or register requirements, and which don't?
5. What may users filter on under Ley 15/2022? Household type and children especially, and whether it matters that the service is offered to the public.
6. Tax on a buy-out between co-owners and on ending a co-ownership, including IRPF and plusvalía.
7. The minimum content of a co-ownership agreement. Is there a template you'd stand behind?
8. Can the right to use particular rooms be registered? What checks are needed before signing arras (title, charges, permitted use, community rules, financing)?
9. How do we protect a group if one borrower drops out before completion?
