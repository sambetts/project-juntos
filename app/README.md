# Compicasa PoC: the working app

The working proof of concept (B-028, B-029), hosted in Azure as if it were production (D-027), in UK South on Sam's existing App Service plan BuildSyncProd and the Azure SQL free offer (D-036). A C# backend with one installable web app for phones and desktops (D-028), filled with made-up people that are refreshed every morning (D-029). Spanish first, English supported (D-024). By the Builder, 2026-11-22.

**Live:** https://app-compicasa-prod-25yr5.azurewebsites.net. It's closed: creating an account needs the invite code (see "Operating it"). Hidden from search engines. Made-up data only: no real users or real personal data without Sam (D-027).

## What it does
- **Accounts (B-034, D-033):** sign up with an email address. In production, sign-up and sign-in happen at **Microsoft Entra External ID** (`compicasa.ciamlogin.com`), which confirms the email with a one-time code and handles forgotten passwords; the app receives only a signed ID token and needs no client secret. Before anyone is sent there, the app checks the invite code and that they ticked "I'm 18 or over" and accepted the terms and privacy notice; those facts travel as a signed 30-minute ticket and are stored with the account (which version, and when). Someone who signs up at External ID without a ticket gets no account here. On a developer's machine and in tests, local email-and-password accounts do the same checks (passwords hashed with ASP.NET Core Identity's hasher). The session is an HTTP-only, SameSite=Strict, secure-only cookie. Delete the account from the profile (removes likes and the groups you started; the External ID sign-in record isn't removed yet: B-039).
- **Onboarding:** after sign-up, an eight-step wizard (name and intro, household, private space, day-to-day habits, dealbreakers, where and how long, home features, summary), saved as you go. Until it's finished the server shows you no one and shows you to no one.
- **Legal pages:** draft terms of use and privacy notice in both languages (`#terms`, `#privacy`), marked as drafts until the lawyer's review and B-020.
- **Navigation (Session 18, B-051):** three destinations, People, Groups and Profile: in the header on wide screens, a bottom tab bar on phones. No permanent bottom bar: it appears only for the onboarding wizard and while you build a group. Every view has an address (`#people-review`, `#people-liked`, `#people-matches`, `#people-passed`, `#person-<id>`, `#groups`, `#group-<id>`, `#group-<id>-brief`, `#group-<id>-homes`, `#preview`, `#profile`), so Back and links work. Plan, second-model review and before/after: `product/2026-11-23-ui-restructure.md`.
- **Profile:** the prototype's v0.3 profile, saved to the database as you change it, plus a name and an optional bio. Each section shows a one-line summary of your answers and opens to edit; the live compatible count sits in the page header. Two examples to start from.
- **People:** three views, *To review*, *Liked* (waiting for them) and *Matches* (mutual), plus *Passed* from a link; someone who says no to a like quietly leaves your list, since likes are silent. Compact cards (two reasons you click, one thing to talk about) 12 at a time; "More" opens the person's sheet. Search, minimum affinity and sort in one row, and the affinity histogram on request (never filtered by household type or money: D-037); dealbreakers first; everyone else ranked by the demo score. Other people's dealbreaker settings, sliders and pickiness are never sent to the browser.
- **Consent:** your like is always explicit. Made-up people reply at once by simulation, as in the prototype (they accept if no dealbreaker applies and the score meets their pickiness). Real people reply with their own likes, so a match needs both to say yes. A like tells a real person, who sees you first in *To review* marked "♥ Liked you"; a fixed sixth of the made-up people who would say yes "like you first" (D-039).
- **Notifications (Session 19, B-037):** a bell in the header with an unread count opens `#alerts`: liked you, match, invitation, yes, declined, left, expired, withdrawn, invited into a free place, formed. Stored per real person (`Notifications`); unread counts for the bell, Groups and Messages come from `/api/inbox/count`, polled every 30 s while the page is visible. No emails yet.
- **Messages (Session 19, B-036):** a fourth destination, *Messages*: a conversation with each mutual match (`#chat-<id>`) and one per group (also its *Chat* view, `#group-<id>-chat`). The code of conduct is agreed once before the first message; 1,000 characters a message, 20 a minute. Made-up people reply at once with scripted lines (worded in the front end). An open conversation refreshes every 10 s. *Report* (reason, note, optionally block) and *Block* on every person sheet and conversation; blocking hides both people from each other and makes the blocker leave shared groups; blocked people are listed under Profile with *Unblock*. Reports are stored; nobody reviews them yet (B-041).
- **Groups:** invitations waiting for your answer first, then groups that could work (from your mutual matches, leaving out groups you already have), then your groups. A group's page has three views: *Overview* (one next step, who's in, how you fit, and links to the brief and the homes), *Home brief* (the home, who gets which rooms, house-agreement starters) and *Homes* (list first; filters folded; a *Market* switch for the stat tiles and charts). Preview the home brief, or propose the group. Made-up members answer at once; real members accept or decline an invitation. A group is "formed" once everyone still in has said yes. **Lifecycle (Session 19, B-038, D-039):** invitations expire after 14 days (checked when someone looks, no background job); whoever sent an invitation, or proposed the group, can withdraw it; someone who declines, leaves, expires or is withdrawn frees a place, and any member who has said yes can invite one of their own mutual matches into it, up to the size the group was proposed at. Rules: `src/Compicasa.Api/Lifecycle.cs`.
- **Home brief:** bedrooms, bathrooms, parking, useful area (editable by the group), must-haves, room allocation, placement tips and house-agreement starters.
- **Homes for sale that fit (D-035, B-046):** a group's page lists homes from our own index that fit its brief: enough bedrooms, a type and an area the group accepts, then a score for bathrooms, size against the group's m² range and the must-haves the advert confirms (price waits for B-043). How it works, at a high level: `product/homes-index.md`. Each home names its agency and has a "See the advert at <agency>" button that opens the agency's own advert in a new tab; groups contact the agency there. Real homes come first, then 90 fixed made-up homes ("Agencia de ejemplo", no link). The made-up homes are on by default (developers' machines and tests) and off on the live site, where `Homes__Demo=false` deletes them at start-up and the endpoint leaves them out (F-019). We store facts and the link only: no photos, description text or agents' contact details. **Explorer (Session 16, B-049):** up to 200 homes that fit come to the browser, which filters them (price, size, bedrooms, type, area, energy, confirmed must-haves, real adverts only), sorts them, and shows stat tiles, a price histogram, a price-against-size scatter with the median €/m² line and the median €/m² by area, all redrawn as filters change; each home has an illustrative floor-plan sketch drawn from its facts. Homes can be filtered by price; people never by household type or money (D-037).
- **Made-up people (D-029):** the 14 hand-written profiles from the prototype, always; plus a batch of 30 generated profiles, replaced each day after 04:00 Madrid time. Anything involving a replaced profile (likes, groups) goes with it. Generated names are common Spanish first names combined at random; bios are assembled from fragments in both languages.
- **Phones:** a PWA: it can be added to the home screen on Android and iOS, opens full screen, and its shell works offline (people and groups always come fresh from the server).

## How it's built
| Part | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Matching engine | The prototype's logic block ported to C#, pure functions. Returns message codes, not sentences, so all wording stays in the front end | `src/Compicasa.Core/Matching.cs` |
| Made-up data | The 14 seed profiles (exported from the prototype) and the daily generator | `src/Compicasa.Core/seed-profiles.json`, `DemoGenerator.cs` |
| API | ASP.NET Core minimal API on .NET 10 (LTS); EF Core on Azure SQL (SQLite locally and in tests) | `src/Compicasa.Api/` |
| Accounts and sign-in | Entra External ID in production, local accounts for developers; the sign-up ticket | `src/Compicasa.Api/Accounts.cs` |
| Schema changes | EF Core migrations for Azure SQL, applied at start-up; the live database was baselined at `Initial` (D-034). SQLite creates its schema from the model: delete `compicasa.db` after a schema change | `src/Compicasa.Api/Schema.cs`, `src/Compicasa.Api/Migrations/` |
| Homes index | Fit rules and Madrid areas (pure); the crawler, robots.txt, the portal block list, the Tecnocasa adapter and the made-up homes | `src/Compicasa.Core/Homes.cs`, `src/Compicasa.Api/Homes.cs` |
| Front end | Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript (no build step), served by the API from the same origin | `src/Compicasa.Api/wwwroot/` |
| Infrastructure | Bicep: one resource group in UK South; the web app runs on the BuildSyncProd plan in its own group | `infra/main.bicep`, `infra/resources.bicep` |
| Deploy | Tests, publish, zip deploy, health wait; idempotent | `infra/deploy.ps1` |

**Azure (rg-compicasa-prod), UK South (D-036):** one web app (Windows, .NET 10, Always On, HTTPS only, TLS 1.2, FTP off, system-assigned managed identity) on Sam's existing App Service plan **BuildSyncProd** (P1v2, in resource group BuildSyncProd, shared with his other apps); Azure SQL Database on the free offer (serverless, 100,000 vCore-seconds and 32 GB a month, pauses instead of billing if that runs out); Entra ID sign-in only, so no database password exists anywhere; Application Insights on a Log Analytics workspace capped at 0.1 GB a day; a €5 monthly budget alert to the subscription's owners. Moved here on 2026-11-22 by deleting and recreating the group (D-036); before that the web app was on F1 in West Europe and the database in France Central (D-030 to D-032). The External ID tenant is in `rg-compicasa-identity`, unchanged.

**Cost:** no extra cost expected. The BuildSyncProd plan is already paid for, the SQL free offer doesn't bill, and logs are capped at about 3 GB a month, inside Log Analytics' free 5 GB per billing account (Reported; the allowance is shared with other workspaces on the same billing account).

**Limits:** the database pauses after 60 idle minutes, so the first visit after a pause can take up to a minute. The web app no longer sleeps (Always On). It shares the plan's CPU and memory with Sam's other apps. The data is in the UK (UK South), which the EU recognises as giving adequate protection to personal data; the draft privacy notice says so.

**Security:** strict Content Security Policy (no inline scripts or styles; colours and sizes are set from script), `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `nosniff`, no referrer, HSTS; every changing API call needs the `X-Compicasa: 1` header (blocks cross-site requests); sign-in and sign-up rate-limited to 10 a minute per address; server-side validation of every profile field; `noindex` on every response. The SQL server accepts connections from Azure services, and sign-in needs an Entra ID identity with a database user (only the web app and the admin have one).

## Run it locally
Needs the .NET 10 SDK. From `app/`:
```
dotnet test
cd src/Compicasa.Api
$env:Auth__InviteCode="local-test"; dotnet run     # PowerShell; uses SQLite (compicasa.db) and local accounts
```
Then open the URL it prints and sign up with any email address and invite code `local-test`.

**Changing the schema:** change the model, then from `src/Compicasa.Api` run `dotnet ef migrations add <Name>` (the `dotnet-ef` tool, version 10). A test fails if you forget.

## Operating it
- **Sign-in set-up (once):** `infra/external-id.ps1` creates the External ID tenant in `rg-compicasa-identity`, the app registration (ID tokens only, no secret), the email sign-up flow, and the web app's `Auth__*` settings. It asks you to sign in to the new tenant once. Against the live site, the browser test stops at the hand-over to External ID, since signing up there needs a real inbox.
- **Deploy:** `infra/deploy.ps1` (both), `-Infra` or `-App`. The app step runs the tests first and won't deploy if they fail, and refuses to deploy until the web app is set up for External ID (D-034). The infrastructure step keeps the invite code and the External ID settings.
- **Invite code:** kept only in the web app's settings, never in this repository. Read it with
  `az webapp config appsettings list -g rg-compicasa-prod -n app-compicasa-prod-25yr5 --subscription 81209232-7da9-4f81-b984-7ab0bd3dcfb3 --query "[?name=='Auth__InviteCode'].value | [0]" -o tsv`.
  Replace it with `deploy.ps1 -Infra -NewInviteCode`. Giving it to anyone but Sam means real users, which needs Sam's decision first (D-027).
- **Crawling homes (D-035, D-036):** the WebJob `homes-sync` (`src/Compicasa.Api/App_Data/jobs/triggered/homes-sync/`) runs every day at 05:00 Madrid time (`settings.job`; the web app's `WEBSITE_TIME_ZONE` is Romance Standard Time). It runs the app in crawl mode, `dotnet Compicasa.Api.dll crawl tecnocasa`, with the web app's own settings and managed identity, so no firewall rule or password is needed. Start it by hand with `az webapp webjob triggered run -g rg-compicasa-prod -n app-compicasa-prod-25yr5 --webjob-name homes-sync`, and see its runs with `az webapp webjob triggered log` (or the portal's WebJobs blade). It waits 3 seconds between requests and fetches only adverts that changed, so the first run takes about two hours and later ones minutes; `WEBJOBS_IDLE_TIMEOUT` is 3600 so a quiet stretch doesn't kill it. Only homes for sale in the province of Madrid. Portals are refused in code (`HomeSources.Portals`), and robots.txt is honoured, including a `User-agent: CompicasaBot` group. The user agent points to `/bot`, which explains what we do and how to block us.
- **Crawling from a developer's machine:** from `src/Compicasa.Api`, after `dotnet build`, run `dotnet bin/Debug/net10.0/Compicasa.Api.dll crawl tecnocasa --Crawl:Max=200` (`--Crawl:DelaySeconds` changes the pause). Locally it uses SQLite. For the live database, allow your IP with a temporary SQL firewall rule, sign in to the Azure CLI as the database admin, and set `Database__Provider=SqlServer`, `Database__Credential=AzureCli`, `Database__TenantId=<the subscription's tenant>` and `ConnectionStrings__Db=Server=tcp:sql-compicasa-uksouth-25yr5.database.windows.net,1433;Database=sqldb-compicasa;Encrypt=True`, with no `Authentication=` part; the app then signs in with a token from `az account get-access-token`. Remove the firewall rule afterwards. (`dotnet run -- crawl …` may be refused by the machine's app control; running the built DLL works.)
- **Stopping an agency:** add its source id to the `Homes__Stopped` app setting (comma-separated, e.g. `tecnocasa`). Its homes disappear from groups at once and the crawler refuses it. To delete its rows too: `DELETE FROM Homes WHERE Source = '<id>'`.
- **Health:** `/healthz` (the app is up) and `/healthz/ready` (it can reach the database; slow if the database is paused).
- **If it needs more room:** it shares BuildSyncProd with Sam's other apps. Give it its own plan by adding one in `infra/resources.bicep` and pointing `planId` at it; if the database pauses too often, drop `useFreeLimit` (it then bills).
- **Logs:** Application Insights `appi-compicasa-prod`.
